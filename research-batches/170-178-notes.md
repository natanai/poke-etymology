# Generation II name audit — #170–#178

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-31  
**Scope:** Chinchou through Xatu  
**Languages:** Japanese, French, English  
**Status:** Every language row manually reviewed under `NAME_EFFECT_STANDARD.md`

## Batch boundary

This batch contains four coherent families:

- Chinchou and Lanturn;
- Pichu, Cleffa, and Igglybuff, which are new pre-evolutions of Generation I families;
- Togepi and Togetic;
- Natu and Xatu.

The block ends at Xatu because #179 begins the separate Mareep family. Review depth, family boundaries, and source quality—not a requirement to use exactly nine species—determined the endpoint.

## Method

For each Japanese, French, and English name:

1. verify the official name and romanization;
2. identify the smallest defensible lexical construction;
3. distinguish documented explanations from plausible reconstructions;
4. apply the blind-name test to the meaning/effect line;
5. move appearance, anatomy, behavior, type, evolution, and lore to Notes or omit them;
6. preserve competing roots when sources or phonology do not justify an exclusive answer;
7. author loanword tags only for exact displayed Roots substrings;
8. review naming provenance separately from lexical etymology.

The factual records use the repository's established current-data convention: current official types and current EV yields, not a simulation of Generation II game mechanics.

## Family decisions

### #170 Chinchou — Loupio — チョンチー (Chonchie)

- Japanese securely points to `提灯` (*chōchin*, lantern).
- `提灯鮟鱇` (*chōchin-ankō*, footballfish) is a plausible more specific source because it begins with the same word, but the available evidence does not prove that the full fish compound was segmented into the species name.
- French `Loupio` is securely based on `loupiote`, a colloquial small lamp. `loupiot`, an affectionate word for a child, remains a secondary echo.
- English `Chinchou` is commonly explained as reordering the main syllables of Japanese *chōchin*. This is plausible rather than documented by an English localizer.

**Rejected overclaims:** anglerfish anatomy, antennae, electrical organs, or underwater lighting do not belong in meaning/effect unless a displayed root encodes them.

### #171 Lanturn — Lanturn — ランターン (Lantern)

- Japanese is a direct adaptation of English `lantern`.
- English `Lanturn` securely respells `lantern`.
- `turn`, especially in the phrase “turn on,” is a plausible secondary pun, not a required independent root.
- French retains the English international spelling instead of rebuilding the name from French `lanterne`.

**Rejected overclaims:** a lantern-shaped body or the act of illuminating the sea is character context, not lexical content.

### #172 Pichu — Pichu — ピチュー (Pichu)

- The secure relationship is contraction of `Pikachu`.
- Japanese `プチ` (*puchi*, petite) and `チューチュー` (*chūchū*, mouse squeak) are plausible internal sound associations, but no primary source reviewed here proves an exact two-root formula.
- French and English retain the Japanese name.
- A later manga character's Pika-plus-Chuchu naming story was explicitly rejected as evidence for the original species name.

**Meaning/effect boundary:** “a shortened Pikachu name” is lexical. Being a baby Pokémon, having small size, or evolving through friendship is not.

### #173 Cleffa — Mélo — ピィ (Py)

- Japanese `Py` is conventionally explained as an extreme clipping of English `pixie`.
- French `Mélo` is explicitly the shortened form of `Mélofée`, whose earlier construction is `mélopée + fée`.
- English `Cleffa` is transparently related to `Clefairy`, but the exact final segmentation is uncertain.
- `clef` and solfège `fa` may reinforce the family's musical vocabulary; neither was upgraded to a uniquely proven construction.

**Promotion:** Cleffa was removed from `reference-data.js` because it is now an authoritative published record in `generation-ii-data.js`.

### #174 Igglybuff — Toudoudou — ププリン (Pupurin)

- Japanese combines `プチ` (*puchi*, petite; ultimately French-derived) with `プリン` (*Purin*, Jigglypuff's Japanese name and also the ordinary borrowed word for pudding).
- French combines `tout doux` and `doudou`; `roudoudou` is retained only as a possible additional sound echo.
- English supports `jiggly` or `wiggly` plus `buff`, while also visibly remodeling `Jigglypuff` and `Wigglytuff`.
- Available evidence does not justify selecting one exclusive English segmentation.

**Promotion:** Igglybuff was removed from `reference-data.js` because the guide now resolves it from the published Generation II factual layer.

### #175 Togepi — Togepi — トゲピー (Togepy)

- Japanese combines `刺` / *toge* (spike) with a clipping of `ピヨピヨ` / *piyopiyo* (a chick's chirping sound).
- French and English retain the Japanese formation.
- Both Japanese roots are tagged in the retained French and English analyses.

**Rejected overclaims:** egg, shell, luck, happiness, and metronome associations are not roots in the displayed name.

### #176 Togetic — Togetic — トゲチック (Togechick)

- The official Japanese romanization `Togechick` makes the construction unusually clear: Japanese `toge` plus English `chick`.
- French and English use the spelling `Togetic`, which obscures the final root but does not create a separate `tic` root.
- The meaning/effect therefore remains “spike chick” across all three languages.

**Rejected overclaims:** angelic form, fairy typing, wings, joy, and evolution are not encoded by these roots.

### #177 Natu — Natu — ネイティ (Naty)

- Japanese `Naty` closely adapts English `native`.
- International `Natu` visually suggests `nature` and may also preserve the Japanese `Naty/native` source.
- No primary naming account reviewed here securely chooses only one international reading.
- The Japanese use of `native` does not itself identify a specific Indigenous nation, artistic tradition, or visual motif.

### #178 Xatu — Xatu — ネイティオ (Natio)

- Japanese `Natio` contains `Naty` and is commonly analyzed with English `native` plus `indio`.
- `indio` exists in Spanish and Italian; the intended donor language and cultural scope are not documented, so the Japanese construction remains plausible rather than confirmed.
- Jeff Kalles specifically identifies English `Xatu` as his suggestion. He says the team sought an X-initial name and that he derived it from `xat`, which he described as a carved Indigenous American pole.
- French retains the English name and therefore uses the documented English explanation rather than inventing an independent French construction.
- `Natu` may reinforce family resemblance, but Kalles's stated `xat` source is the confirmed English core.

**Cultural handling:** the entry reports the coiner's explanation narrowly and attributes the wording to him. It does not infer a totem pole, kachina, quetzal, prophecy, shamanism, or any specific Indigenous culture from the Pokémon's design.

## Naming provenance

Generation II defaults remain generation-scoped and team-level where the exact individual is unknown.

This batch adds one exact override:

- **#178 Xatu, English — Jeff Kalles**, based on his species-specific naming account.

No individual Japanese or French species credit was added. Species-level etymology explanations do not by themselves prove sole coinage.

## Language tags

Tags were authored only where a displayed Roots substring is explicitly borrowed:

- Chinchou English: Japanese `chōchin`;
- Lanturn Japanese and French: English material;
- Pichu French and English: retained Japanese name;
- Cleffa Japanese: English `pixie`;
- Igglybuff Japanese: French-derived `プチ`;
- Togepi French and English: Japanese `toge` and `piyopiyo`;
- Togetic: English `chick` in Japanese, and the inherited Japanese/English roots in French and English;
- Natu Japanese: English `native`;
- Xatu Japanese: English `native`; French: retained English `Xatu`.

The possible `indio` component is deliberately not tagged because the evidence does not resolve whether Spanish or Italian supplied the form. A tag records donor-language status; it does not upgrade an uncertain root or donor language to certainty.

- publishes them in `generation-ii-data.js`;
- removes their duplicate records from `reference-data.js`;
- leaves all newer guide references intact;
- expands `.github/workflows/validate-guide.yml` so changes to `data.js`, `generated-data.js`, `generation-ii-data.js`, or `reference-data.js` trigger the guide validator.

This closes a validation gap: shared factual-data changes can no longer bypass Living Dex checks merely because no file under `guides/` changed.
