# Research notes — #145–#151

Reviewed: 2026-07-30

This is the final Generation I audit block: Zapdos through Mew.

## Scope and method

Each Japanese, French, and English analysis was reviewed independently. Dictionaries establish meanings and borrowing status; localizer and naming-team testimony establishes intent only where the source actually discusses the naming decision. Proper-name retention, design credit, and lexical origin remain separate claims.

## #145 Zapdos

- Japanese サンダー is the English loanword *thunder* and receives an exact entry-owned tag.
- French Électhor is *électrique + Thor*. Julien Bardakoff describes the French legendary birds as a coordinated element-plus-deity set, so the root is confirmed and a narrow French naming-credit override is justified.
- English Zapdos is *zap + Spanish dos*. Bill Giese directly recalls the uno/dos/tres pattern, so *dos* receives an English-analysis loanword tag.

## #146 Moltres

- Japanese ファイヤー is the English loanword *fire* and receives an exact tag.
- French Sulfura combines sulfurous volcanic language with the sun god Râ. The same Bardakoff trio explanation supports a narrow French naming-credit override.
- English Moltres is *molten + Spanish tres*. *Molt* is retained only as a secondary sound association; *tres* receives an exact loanword tag.

## #147 Dratini

- Japanese ミニリュウ is *mini + dragon*. ミニ is secure English borrowing and receives a tag.
- French Minidraco is *miniature/mini + Latin draco* according to localizer commentary.
- English Dratini most naturally compresses *dragon + tiny/teeny*, but the exact clipping is not documented, so the claim remains plausible.
- The early English promotional name *Dragoon* is useful development context, not a root of the final name.

## #148 Dragonair

- Japanese ハクリュー is the ordinary compound 白竜, “white dragon.”
- French Draco is Latin *draco*, confirmed in localizer commentary.
- English most clearly presents *dragon + air*, but *debonair* and *lair* are credible overlapping associations. No source selects one exclusive second element, so the analysis remains plausible.

## #149 Dragonite

- Japanese カイリュー is the direct sea-dragon compound 海竜. Other sound associations such as 怪獣, 快, and 破壊 remain Notes only.
- French Dracolosse is *draco + colosse*, confirmed in localizer commentary.
- English may combine *dragon* with *knight*, the suffix *-ite*, or the legendary stone *draconite*. *Night* is another sound overlap. The evidence does not justify choosing one as certain.

## #150 Mewtwo

- The direct structure is Mew + *two* in all three languages.
- Japanese ツー is an English loanword and receives a tag.
- French retains the international proper name; the entire name is not treated as an ordinary French lexical borrowing.
- Clone naming in *The Birth of Mewtwo* makes the duplicate/successor function of *-two* explicit. It does not refer to National Pokédex number 150.

## #151 Mew

- Localizer testimony reports that the English cat cry *mew* was explicitly rejected as the intended source.
- The supported explanation connects ミュウ with ミュータント, “mutant.” English *mew* and Japanese 妙 are retained only as secondary associations.
- Shigeki Morimoto designed and programmed Mew, but available sources do not separately state that he personally coined the final word. The Japanese Name credit therefore remains the documented Game Freak naming staff rather than inferring naming authorship from design credit.
- French and English retain the Japanese proper name unchanged.

## Language tags added

- #145 Japanese `サンダー` — English
- #145 English `dos` — Spanish
- #146 Japanese `ファイヤー` — English
- #146 English `tres` — Spanish
- #147 Japanese `ミニ` — English
- #150 Japanese `ツー` — English

Classical roots, scientific vocabulary, retained proper names, and uncertain sound overlaps were not tagged.

## Naming-credit decisions

- Add Julien Bardakoff exact French overrides for Électhor and Sulfura because his recorded legendary-bird explanation presents the deity pattern as his coordinated localization decision.
- Retain Bill Giese’s existing exact English overrides for Zapdos and Moltres.
- Do not add a Shigeki Morimoto name-credit override for Mew: creation/design/programming evidence is not the same as explicit name coinage.
