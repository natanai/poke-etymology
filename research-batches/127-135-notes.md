# Research decisions: #127–#135

Reviewed 2026-07-30.

This batch covers Pinsir through Jolteon. It was researched in three coherent groups: Pinsir/Tauros, Magikarp through Eevee, and the first two Eevee evolutions.

## Source approach

The normal base source set is supplemented with:

- Julien Bardakoff's first-person explanations of Léviator and the Évoli/Aquali/Voltali/Pyroli naming system;
- Ken Sugimori's archived statement that Gyarados has no specific lexical origin and was selected for its force;
- the 2018 *EVs* artbook account that Eevee/Eievui pronounce E-V, the opening letters of *evolution*;
- dictionaries and historical references for Ancient Greek, French, Japanese, scientific, and mythological context.

A transparent dictionary split is not treated as confirmation of naming intent by itself. Conversely, a creator or localizer statement is not stretched beyond what it actually says.

## #127 Pinsir / Scarabrute / Kailios

- Japanese is treated as compressed `怪力` (superhuman strength) plus Ancient Greek `κοριός` (bug).
- The Greek word is broader than “stag beetle,” and the exact name seam is not perfectly visible, so confidence remains strong rather than confirmed.
- French `scarabée + brute` follows localizer documentation.
- English is a deliberate reshaping of `pincer / pincher`.
- No loanword tag is added because the Japanese Greek boundary is compressed and the project does not tag uncertain foreign resemblance.

## #128 Tauros / Tauros / Kentauros

- Japanese primarily shortens `ケンタウロス`, the standard form of *centaur*.
- `拳` (*ken*, fist) is retained as a reported secondary association, not promoted to the sole root.
- French and English use Ancient Greek `ταῦρος` (bull).
- Latin *Taurus* and the zodiac sign are useful English associations but are not substituted for the closer Greek spelling.
- No loanword tag is used for mythological/classical name material.

## #129 Magikarp / Magicarpe / Koiking

- Japanese is `鯉 + キング` and receives an exact English-loanword tag over `キング`.
- A possible echo of `金魚` (*kingyo*, goldfish) remains secondary.
- French and English independently use magic + carp.
- The grand magical or royal language is explained as both comic contrast and evolution foreshadowing.

## #130 Gyarados / Léviator / Gyarados

This is the batch's most important correction to common fan etymology.

- Ken Sugimori stated that `ギャラドス` has no specific origin and was chosen for its force.
- Later similarities to Japanese words for slaughter, adversity, reversal, devastation, and threatening are retained in Notes only.
- English is confirmed as deliberate retention of the Japanese name after the proposed `Skulkraken` failed legal review; Nob Ogasawara recommended keeping Gyarados.
- French `Léviathan + or` follows localizer documentation.
- Julien Bardakoff receives a narrow French exact-name credit because he describes choosing Léviator in first person; this does not replace the broader Nintendo France team default elsewhere.

## #131 Lapras / Lokhlass / Laplace

- Japanese is linked to the surname of Pierre-Simon Laplace.
- The evidence reports the association but does not explain why the mathematician was selected, so the claim remains strong rather than overextended.
- French combines Loch Ness with the enthusiastic expression `la classe !`.
- English closely adapts the Japanese proper name.
- A possible French-sounding `la place` passenger pun remains secondary.
- Proper names do not receive loanword tags.

## #132 Ditto / Métamorph / Metamon

- Japanese is analyzed as clipped English `metamorphosis + monster`.
- Exact tags are authored over `メタ` and `モン`.
- French uses the ordinary shape-changing term `métamorphe`; Notes preserve Bardakoff's *Gandahar* association.
- English uses the existing word `ditto`, naming the copied result rather than the transformation process.

## #133 Eevee / Évoli / Eievui

- The *EVs* artbook confirms that Eevee and Eievui pronounce E-V, the first two letters of *evolution*.
- This is letter-name wordplay rather than an ordinary lexical borrowing, so no loanword tag is used.
- French combines evolution/evolving, volition, and the shared `-li` family element associated by Bardakoff with Greek `lithos` (stone).
- Motofumi Fujiwara's design and name-origin explanation is not converted into an individual naming credit because the source does not explicitly say he coined the final name.
- Bardakoff receives a French family-name override because he explains the coordinated system in first person.

## #134 Vaporeon / Aquali / Showers

- Japanese is the English borrowing `シャワーズ` and receives an exact tag.
- French is Latin `aqua` plus the coordinated `-li` family ending.
- English is `vapor + -eon`; the ending is treated primarily as a family marker rather than forced into the ordinary time-span meaning of *eon*.
- Bardakoff receives the documented French family-name override.

## #135 Jolteon / Voltali / Thunders

- Japanese is the English borrowing `サンダース` and receives an exact tag.
- French is `volt + -li`.
- English is `jolt + -eon`.
- Bardakoff receives the documented French family-name override.

## Naming-credit override beyond the audited range

The same first-person source explicitly covers Pyroli as part of the original French Évoli family. A French Bardakoff override is therefore also added for #136 even though Flareon's etymology remains pending until the next audit batch. Attribution and etymology audit status are separate.

## Entry-owned tags added

Five tags were authored across four language analyses:

- #129 Japanese `キング`
- #132 Japanese `メタ`
- #132 Japanese `モン`
- #134 Japanese `シャワーズ`
- #135 Japanese `サンダース`

No tags were added for:

- compressed or uncertain Greek material in Kailios;
- centaur/Tauros classical name material;
- Laplace as a proper name;
- Eevee's E-V letter-name construction;
- learned Latin/Greek roots in the French Eevee family;
- English roots inside English analyses.
