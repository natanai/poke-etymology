# Research notes — #073–#081

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-30  
**Range:** Tentacruel, Geodude, Graveler, Golem, Ponyta, Rapidash, Slowpoke, Slowbro, Magnemite

## Scope and verification

- Confirmed the repository’s official English, French, Japanese, and trademark-style romanized names against the current structured records and the standard official/PokéAPI source set.
- Confirmed the current canonical types and EV-yield records already present in `generated-data.js`; this batch changes research overlays only.
- Preserved the required language order in every entry: Japanese, French, English.
- Added targeted sources only where they materially improve reliability: an in-game PokéQuiz transcript, dictionaries, a cultural reference for *golem*, Julien Bardakoff’s French-name explanation, and Jadranka Stojaković’s interview.

## Reliability decisions by family

### #073 Tentacruel

- Japanese `Dokukurage` is treated as **strong** for `doku` + `kurage`. `Doku` directly matches the Poison type, so the more elaborate possible echo of `me no doku` is kept only as contextual family wordplay rather than promoted into the root line.
- French is not described merely as “same as English.” `tentacule` and `cruel` are recognizable French words, so the international spelling continues to function as French wordplay even though the name was retained.

### #074–#076 Geodude family

- Geodude’s Japanese and English analyses are **confirmed**, not merely strong. Pokémon Black 2 / White 2’s PokéQuiz explicitly explains `ishi` + `tsubute` and `geo` + `dude`.
- `geode` remains a possible visual echo for English Geodude, but it is not allowed to displace the official `geo` + `dude` explanation.
- French `Racaillou` is **strong** for `racaille` + `caillou`; the Notes preserve the pejorative, streetwise register of `racaille` rather than flattening it to “rock.”
- Graveler’s French first element remains **plausible** because references alternate between `gravier` and `gravats`. Both yield the same loose-rock/rubble image with `avalanche`, and the published entry records the unresolved alternative rather than choosing arbitrarily.
- Japanese `Golone` and `Golonya` remain **plausible**. The rolling-rock sound symbolism is persuasive, but the compressed English `stone` ending and Italian `montagna` reading are not primary-source-confirmed.
- Golem’s English root is transparent, but its Notes distinguish the Jewish cultural tradition from the generic “stone monster” meaning common in modern fantasy. This prevents an etymology entry from silently erasing the word’s cultural history.

### #077–#078 Ponyta family

- Ponyta is **plausible**, not strong, for `pony` + `bonita`. `pony` is obvious, but no creator statement confirms the second component, and the stress pattern is imperfect.
- French retains Ponyta rather than generating a separate French construction; the Notes explain that French normally spells the animal `poney` and does not independently supply `bonita`.
- Rapidash is **strong** for `rapid` + `dash`; `ash` is retained only as a secondary Fire-type echo.
- French `Galopa` is based on `galop`/`galopade`. The Notes add the native-language association with the literary past form `il/elle galopa` and the family rhyme with Ponyta without claiming either as an officially documented root.

### #079–#080 Slowpoke family

- Japanese `Yadon` remains **plausible** and deliberately unresolved. Candidate associations include `yadokari`, `yadonushi`, `don`, and shortening from `Yadoran`.
- The possibility that Yadon was back-formed from Yadoran is important because Yadoran has unusually direct personal-name evidence.
- Jadranka Stojaković’s published interview is treated as sufficient primary testimony to mark the Yadoran personal-name claim **confirmed**. The Notes preserve the Japanese host/hermit-crab/dullness echoes as secondary associations rather than pretending the direct account eliminates them phonetically.
- French `Ramoloss` and `Flagadoss` are **confirmed** from Julien Bardakoff’s explanation: `ramollo` / `flagada` plus a nickname-forming `-ss`. Older fan analyses deriving the endings from `molosse` are intentionally rejected.
- English `slowpoke` is treated as an existing informal word, while `Slowbro` is the transparent `slow` + `bro` construction.

### #081 Magnemite

- Japanese `Coil` is **strong** and contextualized as technical electrical vocabulary, especially an electromagnetic coil.
- French `Magnéti` is **strong** for `magnétique` + `petit`.
- English is **plausible** for `magnet` + `mite`. `might`, `magnetite`, and `maghemite` are retained as secondary sound associations because no primary evidence chooses among the possible endings.

## Source-quality notes

- Bulbapedia and Poképédia were used as structured research leads and cross-language summaries, not as automatic proof.
- The Geodude confidence upgrade rests on quoted in-game text, not repetition across fan databases.
- The Slowbro Japanese claim rests on Stojaković’s own account in a published interview.
- The Slowpoke-family French claims rest on the original localizer’s explanation, which also resolves a long-repeated but weaker `molosse` theory.
- Dictionaries support literal meanings and register; they do not by themselves prove naming intent.

## Unresolved readings preserved

- Graveler: `gravier` versus `gravats` in French.
- Golone: exact contribution of English `stone` beyond the final sound.
- Golonya: whether Italian `montagna` was intentionally used.
- Ponyta: whether `bonita` was intended rather than the ending simply making `pony` name-like.
- Yadon: host/hermit-crab/dullness cluster versus shortening from Yadoran.
- Magnemite: `mite` versus secondary `might` and mineral echoes.
