# Generation II name audit: #161–#169

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-31  
**Scope:** Sentret through Crobat, across Japanese, French, and English = **27 language analyses**

## Method

Each language was researched independently. Official names and romanizations were checked against the project's current PokeAPI-backed naming convention, then name-origin summaries were compared across Bulbapedia and Poképédia. Ordinary lexical roots were checked against Japanese, French, English, Spanish, and classical-reference sources where useful.

Every meaning/effect line was written using the blind-name test from `NAME_EFFECT_STANDARD.md`: the line must remain derivable after hiding the sprite, anatomy, behavior, evolution method, type, mechanics, and Pokédex story. Design and behavioral context belongs only in Notes or localization comparison.

## Batch structure

This is the second nine-species Generation II batch:

- #161 Sentret / Fouinette / Otachi
- #162 Furret / Fouinar / Ootachi
- #163 Hoothoot / Hoothoot / Hoho

## Important decisions

### Sentret and Furret

- **Otachi** is best treated as likely `尾` (tail) + `立ち` (standing), with `鼬` (*itachi*, weasel) as overlapping wordplay rather than a separately proven exclusive origin.
- **Ootachi** transparently adds `大` (*ō*, big) to the earlier name. The meaning/effect line therefore says only “A big Otachi”; Notes preserve the inherited tail/standing/weasel texture.
- **Fouinette** most securely combines *fouine* with diminutive `-ette`; *belette* may reinforce the ending.
- **Fouinar** has conflicting secondary summaries: Poképédia gives *fouine + renard*, while Bulbapedia gives *fouine + fouiner*. Both remain available in Roots and Notes; neither is promoted to certainty.
- **Sentret** and **Furret** are straightforward `sentry + ferret` and `fur + ferret` blends.

### Hoothoot and Noctowl

- **Hoho / Hoothoot** is simply a doubled owl call in all three languages. Clock and cuckoo associations remain secondary Notes material and do not enter the gloss.
- **Yorunozuku** is `夜` (night) plus a clipped form of *mimizuku* (horned owl).
- **Noarfang** is the documented *noir + harfang* construction. The lexical result is “black snowy owl”; whether the design is literally black is irrelevant to the name's meaning.
- **Noctowl** is `nocturnal + owl` without added wisdom, eyesight, or nocturnal-behavior claims.

### Ledyba and Ledian

- **Rediba / Ledyba** most plausibly combines *ladybug* or *ladybird* with *red*. The exact clipping boundary and choice between *ladybug* and *ladybird* are not documented, so confidence remains plausible.
- **Coxy** is a documented clipping of *coccinelle*. Possible accidental echoes such as English *sexy* or anatomical *coxa* were rejected because the localizer explanation does not require them.
- **Redian / Ledian** retains the ladybug/ladybird and red family roots. Reference works also propose *alien* or *guardian* for the ending, but neither is documented strongly enough to select. The gloss explicitly calls the ending unresolved.
- **Coxyclaque** is the documented `coccinelle + claque` blend. The Pokémon's hands explain why the joke fits but do not belong in meaning/effect.

### Spinarak and Ariados

- **Itomaru** is `糸` (thread) + `丸` (circle and a traditional name ending). Both the round lexical sense and name-like texture are preserved.
- **Mimigal** is documented `mimi + mygale`, giving “cute tarantula.”
- **Spinarak** is best explained by *spin* or *spinneret + arachnid*. German *Spinne* and the Afrikaans word *spinnerak* are retained only as secondary sound matches.
- **Ariados** is the batch's most uncertain name. Ariadne's guiding thread through the Labyrinth is the strongest cultural association. Other proposed segmentations include *arachnid + 脅す* (*odosu*, threaten) and Spanish *dos* (two). The data records all of these as possibilities and refuses to invent a single confirmed split.
- **Migalos** is documented `mygale + colosse`, “a colossal tarantula.”

### Crobat

- **Crobat** most visibly permits `cross + bat`. Nearly the whole word also overlaps *acrobat*, and Japanese `黒` (*kuro*, black) may contribute to the opening sound. No primary source selects one exclusive reading, so both Japanese and English remain plausible rather than strong or confirmed.
- **Nostenfer** is documented `Nosferatu + enfer`, “a Nosferatu-and-hell blend.”
- Flight ability, wing arrangement, high Speed, friendship evolution, poison typing, and vampire-bat biology were excluded from meaning/effect because none is lexical meaning.

## Attribution

No new exact individual name-credit override was added. Poképédia's localizer-linked etymology notes support the French constructions, but they do not independently establish species-by-species sole coinage beyond the already documented Generation II Nintendo France team context. Japanese and English likewise retain the Generation II scoped defaults.

## Validation and scaling improvements

This batch also removes two fixed #160 assumptions:

- region-filter validation now derives the published Johto IDs from runtime data and tests a new `johto bug` query;
- naming-credit validation now enforces contiguous published IDs and complete three-language coverage without hard-coding the current maximum.

These changes mean future Generation II batches expand both validators automatically rather than requiring fragile expected-list edits.

## Preserved boundaries

The batch changes factual names, types, current EV yields, audited language research, Notes, sources, tags, documentation, and validation coverage only. It does not alter FireRed/LeafGreen guide stages, tasks, saved-state schema, encounter data, or guide localization.
