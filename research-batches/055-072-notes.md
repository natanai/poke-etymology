# Audit notes: #055–#072

Reviewed 2026-07-30 under `RESEARCH_METHOD.md`.

- **Golduck / Golduck:** `gold + duck` is clear, but the reason for *gold* is not. The entry does not claim literal color; psychic brilliance, prestige, and value remain contextual possibilities.
- **Akwakwak:** `aquatique + couac` is transparent. The ordinary French double sense of *couac*—a quacking/discordant sound and a blunder—is retained.
- **Mankey:** Japanese supports `man + monkey`; English also permits *mad* and *cranky* echoes. These are preserved as overlapping possibilities rather than collapsed into one root.
- **Primeape:** Japanese `怒り猿`, French `colosse + singe`, and English `prime/primate + ape` are all comparatively strong.
- **Growlithe / Arcanine:** the Japanese family shifts from guardian to wind-speed imagery; French and English independently converge on canine vocabulary.
- **Poliwag family:** `nyoronyoro` is secure sound symbolism. The child/youngster/boy endings are plausible but compressed. French clearly escalates from little tadpole to slap-based fighting wordplay.
- **Abra family:** Japanese person-name references remain *plausible*, not confirmed. French and English `Abra → Kadabra → Alakazam` are treated as a clear stage-magic sequence.
- **Machop family:** Japanese uses established strength words (`腕力`, `剛力`, `怪力`). French progresses from impact to grabbing to a heavy hitter; English progresses from chop to choke to champion.
- **Bellsprout family:** `まだ蕾` is a natural Japanese phrase. `Utsudon` securely shortens the pitcher-plant term; the melancholy echo is secondary. `Utsubot` probably adds *pot*, but the compressed boundary remains plausible.
- **Tentacool:** `kurage` (jellyfish) is secure. The proposed Japanese first element may involve either `目の敵` or `目の毒`; neither is promoted above the other.
