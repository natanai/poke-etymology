# Generation II audit #152–#160

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-31  
**Scope:** Chikorita, Bayleef, Meganium; Cyndaquil, Quilava, Typhlosion; Totodile, Croconaw, Feraligatr

## Batch boundary

The first Generation II batch uses the three Johto starter families because they form three complete, linguistically coherent lines while remaining small enough to test the repository's Generation II data, attribution, semantic-baseline, and merge workflow before larger batches.

The branch deliberately does not modify any `guides/` file. FireRed Living Dex work may proceed concurrently and must be incorporated from the latest `main` before this PR is merged.

## Data architecture

Generation I remains generated through `generated-data.js`. The first Johto records live in `generation-ii-data.js`, which appends only #152–#160 after Generation I has been assembled. This prevents the Generation I deployment builder from overwriting Johto work and avoids expanding the network-backed build script during the pilot batch.

Only researched Johto species are published in this batch. The remaining Generation II species are not bulk-filled with guessed etymology or empty public records.

## Name-effect review

All 27 Japanese, French, and English meaning/effect lines were written under `NAME_EFFECT_STANDARD.md` and reviewed with the blind-name test.

Examples of deliberately excluded context:

- Cyndaquil's flames and their bodily placement do not appear in the English gloss; `cinder + quill` is glossed only as a cinder-and-quill blend.
- Feraligatr's size and anatomy are not inferred from its design. The Japanese gloss includes large only because 大 is one plausible reading of the name's opening sound.
- Quilava's historical selection as the Q-name is recorded in Notes and naming credit, not treated as lexical meaning.
- evolutionary-stage language is kept in localization comparison rather than inserted into Roots-only glosses.

## Family decisions

### Chikorita family

- **Chikorita / Chicorita:** chicory plus Spanish diminutive `-ita` is treated as strong. English retains the Japanese construction with `c` changed to `k`.
- **Germignon:** `germe + mignon` is transparent and treated as confirmed through French-localizer documentation.
- **Bayleef / Bayleaf:** direct bay-leaf wordplay; English respells `leaf` as `leef`.
- **Macronium:** `macro- + géranium`, forming a French bridge to Méganium.
- **Meganium / Méganium:** `mega- + geranium` is transparent across all three languages.

### Cyndaquil family

- **Hinoarashi:** `火 + ヤマアラシ` is the primary analysis, with the full-name echo of `火の嵐` recorded as additional phrase-level wordplay.
- **Héricendre:** `hérisson + cendre`.
- **Cyndaquil:** `cinder + quill`.
- **Magmarashi:** `magma + yama-arashi` is primary; `anaguma` remains a secondary plausible overlap.
- **Quilava:** `quill + lava`. Jeff Kalles's documented authorship is recorded separately from lexical analysis.
- **Bakphoon / Typhlosion:** Japanese combines `爆風` with borrowed `typhoon`; French and English make `typhoon + explosion` available directly. Typhon remains only a possible learned echo.

### Totodile family

- **Waninoko:** the native phrase `鰐の子`, literally a crocodile's child.
- **Kaiminus:** `caïman + minus`, with `minuscule` as reinforcement.
- **Totodile:** `tot + crocodile`.
- **Alligates:** shortened borrowed English `alligator`; a separate `gates` root is not asserted.
- **Crocrodil:** `croc + crocodile`, also preserving a childlike repeated pronunciation.
- **Croconaw:** `crocodile + gnaw`.
- **Ordile:** the opening `ō` may evoke king, emperor, or large; the katakana spelling does not choose one kanji, so the gloss preserves all three cautiously.
- **Aligatueur:** `alligator + tueur`.
- **Feraligatr:** `feral` or `ferocious + alligator`; the missing letters are explained by the historical ten-character English name limit.

## Language tags

Loanword tags are authored only where the receiving-language analysis explicitly establishes borrowed material with a supportable token boundary. New tags cover Japanese or English use of English, and Spanish `-ita` where applicable.

## Naming credits

Generation II has its own defaults rather than inheriting Generation I silently:

- Japanese remains a cautious Game Freak team-level credit.
- French uses the documented first-251 Nintendo France context and remains team-level unless a species-specific decision is documented.
- English uses the credited Pokémon Gold and Silver US localization coordinators as a team-level default.
- English Quilava has a specific Jeff Kalles override because he identifies it as his suggestion.

## Sources and uncertainty

Each entry retains the standard official Pokédex, PokéAPI, and specialist-reference base list plus targeted dictionaries or historical sources. Specialist Pokémon references are used as research summaries and leads; ordinary word meanings are supported by dictionaries where useful.

The most important unresolved boundaries are Magmarashi's animal ending, Typhlosion's secondary Typhon echo, and Ordile's opening `ō`. These remain locally marked plausible rather than converted into false certainty.
