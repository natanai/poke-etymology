## Reliability decisions: #091–#099

### #091 Cloyster

- Japanese `Parshen` is **strong** for `pearl + shell`.
- French `Crustabri` is **strong** for `crustacé + abri`; Notes explicitly acknowledge that the visual category is not strict zoological taxonomy.
- English is **strong** for `cloister + oyster`. `clam` is retained only as a visual association because it is not needed to explain the complete wordplay.

### #092–#094 Gastly family

- `Ghos` and `Ghost` are treated as clipped and full forms of the English loanword *ghost*.
- French `Fantominus`, `Spectrum`, and `Ectoplasma` are analyzed as deliberate mock-Latin or scientific-sounding constructions rather than flattened to generic ghost words.
- English `Gastly` and `Haunter` are transparent.
- Japanese `Gangar` and English `Gengar` remain **plausible** for a `-gänger` / doppelgänger or related revenant association. The popular doppelgänger explanation is not marked confirmed because the surviving evidence does not uniquely select it from the broader foreign-root cluster.

### #095 Onix

- French and English are **strong** for a respelling of *onyx*.
- Japanese `Iwark` securely begins with `岩` (*iwa*, rock), but its ending remains unresolved between serpent and fortification imagery. The entry records both possibilities without pretending either is primary-source-confirmed.

### #096–#097 Drowzee family

- Japanese `Sleepe` and `Sleeper` are direct English loans.
- French `Soporifik` is grounded in *soporifique*, while `Hypnomade` transparently combines hypnosis and a nomad.
- English `Drowzee` preserves the conventional written-sleep association of Z/zzz; `Hypno` uses the learned hypnosis root rather than an ordinary standalone noun.

### #098–#099 Krabby family

- Japanese `Crab` is a direct loan rather than a hidden construction.
- English `Krabby` is **strong** for `crab + crabby`; French retains the international name, where the animal remains recoverable but the temperament joke depends on English.
- `Kingler` remains **plausible** for `king crab + fiddler crab`. Both animals fit the final form, especially its unequal claw, but the clipping is too compressed to call certain.
- French `Krabboss` is **strong** for the family name plus *boss*.

## Reliability decisions: #100–#108

### #100–#101 Voltorb family

- Japanese `Biriridama` is **strong** for electric-shock mimetic language plus `玉` (*tama*, ball), voiced as *dama*.
- French and English Voltorb are parallel `volt + orb/orbe` constructions.
- Japanese `Marumine` is **strong** for `丸` (*maru*, round) + *mine*.
- French and English `Électrode / Electrode` are treated as the existing scientific word. A resemblance to *explode* is contextual and not promoted into a required root.

### #102–#103 Exeggcute family

- Japanese `Tamatama` preserves both repeated `玉` (*tama*, ball) and the ordinary word `たまたま` (‘by chance’). The egg association through *tamago* is noted without replacing the complete-word pun.
- French `Noeunoeuf` follows Julien Bardakoff’s documented egg repetition plus *neuneu* explanation and is marked **confirmed**.
- English `Exeggcute` is **strong** for *execute* reshaped around *egg* and *cute*.
- Japanese `Nassy` remains **plausible** for coconut/nuts plus `椰子` (*yashi*, palm). The audible *nashi* ‘pear’ association is secondary because it explains the design less well.
- French `Noadkoko` is **confirmed** as a phonetic reshaping of *noix de coco*.
- English `Exeggutor` continues the execute/executor family while inserting egg.

### #104–#105 Cubone family

- Japanese `Karakara → Garagara` is treated as a sound-symbolic escalation from light dry clatter to heavier rattling.
- French `Osselait → Ossatueur` follows documented little-bone/milk and little-bone/killer constructions.
- English progresses transparently from `cub + bone` to `marrow + whack`.

### #106–#107 Hitmon pair

- Japanese `Sawamular` and `Ebiwalar` remain **plausible** references to Tadashi Sawamura and Hiroyuki Ebihara. The athlete matches are persuasive, but the exact agent-ending segmentation lacks a direct creator statement.
- French `Kicklee` and `Tygnon` use documented localizer explanations and are **confirmed**: kick + Bruce Lee, and Mike Tyson + *gnon*.
- English person-name tributes to Bruce Lee and Jackie Chan are **strong**. The shared `hit + mon(ster)` family frame remains plausible support rather than being treated as equally documented at every boundary.

### #108 Lickitung

- Japanese `Beroringa` securely evokes `べろ` (*bero*, tongue) and `べろり` (*berori*, licking), while the likely Latin *lingua* ending remains **plausible**.
- French `Excelangue` follows the localizer-supported `excellent + excès de langue` explanation and is **confirmed**.
- English `lick + tongue` is **confirmed** because Pokémon Black 2 / White 2’s in-game PokéQuiz explicitly states the two components.
