# English name-effect corrections

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-30

## Problem identified

The English meaning/effect line should explain what the English name construction itself communicates: its literal image, ordinary-word meaning, joke, tone, or morphological effect.

Some audited entries instead imported facts from the Pokémon's body, evolutionary status, or lore. Those facts may explain why a name was selected, but they are not necessarily conveyed by the English word or roots themselves. This was clearest in Farfetch'd: *far-fetched* means implausible or difficult to believe; it does not literally mean a duck carrying a vegetable.

## Corrected entries

- #078 Rapidash — removed the horse identity from the gloss; retained rapid + dash and the possible ash echo.
- #083 Farfetch'd — replaced the duck-and-vegetable description with the ordinary meaning of *far-fetched*.
- #109 Koffing — removed the gas-creature premise; retained coughing and cough-sound spelling.
- #110 Weezing — removed the gas-creature premise; retained the meaning of *wheezing*.
- #114 Tangela — removed vines from the gloss; retained *tangle* and the name-like ending.
- #138 Omanyte — removed fossil revival lore; retained the reshaped *ammonite* effect.
- #140 Kabuto — removed fossil-creature framing; retained the borrowed Japanese helmet and horseshoe-crab associations.
- #141 Kabutops — removed evolved-fossil framing; retained Kabuto and the unresolved prehistoric-sounding ending.
- #150 Mewtwo — removed genetic-engineering lore; retained the direct Mew + two construction.

## Boundary used for the audit

Creature-oriented wording was not removed merely because a gloss applied a root to the Pokémon. The line was changed only when the added design or story information materially exceeded what the English construction communicates. Roots, confidence labels, Notes, comparisons, sources, and naming credits were left unchanged.
