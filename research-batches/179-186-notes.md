# Generation II name audit — #179–#186

**Reviewed:** 2026-08-08  
**Scope:** Mareep through Politoed  
**Languages:** Japanese, French, English  
**Status:** Every language row manually reviewed under `NAME_EFFECT_STANDARD.md`

## Batch boundary

This batch contains:

- Mareep, Flaaffy, and Ampharos;
- Bellossom, a Generation II branch evolution of a Generation I family;
- Marill and Azumarill;
- Sudowoodo;
- Politoed, a Generation II branch evolution of a Generation I family.

The batch ends at #186 because #187 starts the separate Hoppip family. Bellossom and Politoed were already used by the Living Dex as compact references, so publishing them here also exercises the repository's reference-promotion path.

## Method

For every Japanese, French, and English row:

1. verify the official name and official romanization;
2. identify the smallest defensible lexical construction;
3. separate documented explanations from structural reconstructions;
4. preserve competing roots when the spelling does not select one uniquely;
5. apply the blind-name test to meaning/effect;
6. move appearance, behavior, typing, evolution, mechanics, and story context to Notes or omit them;
7. author loanword tags only for exact displayed Roots substrings with a supportable donor language;
8. review naming provenance separately from etymology.

Current types and EV yields follow the repository's existing modern-data convention. The factual values were checked against PokeAPI's current `pokemon_types.csv` and `pokemon_stats.csv`, not reconstructed from Generation II mechanics.

## Family decisions

### #179 Mareep — Wattouat — メリープ (Merriep)

- Japanese has several overlapping published readings: English `merry`, `メー` (*mē*, a sheep's baa), `Mary`, and English `sheep`.
- The spelling does not prove which of merry/Mary/baa contributed independently, so Japanese remains plausible rather than being flattened to one neat formula.
- French `Wattouat` combines `watt` and `ouate`; the words are pronounced alike, making the blend unusually transparent.
- English `Mareep` is commonly read as `Mary + sheep` and is exactly an anagram of `ampere`.
- The nursery-rhyme context explains the proposed `Mary` root; it is not used as evidence for appearance or character behavior.

### #180 Flaaffy — Lainergie — モココ (Mokoko)

- Japanese is commonly reconstructed from `羊毛` (*yōmō*, wool) and `もこもこ` (*mokomoko*, fluffy/billowy).
- French `Lainergie` is `laine + énergie`.
- English `Flaaffy` is commonly `fluffy + baa`.
- The doubled `aa` may additionally suggest `AA`, but that battery-like reading remains secondary because the fluffy/baa blend already accounts for the name.

### #181 Ampharos — Pharamp — デンリュウ (Denryu)

- Japanese directly matches `電流` (*denryū*, electric current) and simultaneously permits the homophonic `電竜` (*den ryū*, electric dragon).
- `電球` (*denkyū*, light bulb) is a published possible additional echo, not required for the core analysis.
- French `Pharamp` is `phare + ampère`.
- English `Ampharos` is `amp/ampere + Greek pharos` (lighthouse).
- Spanish/Portuguese `amparo` is sometimes proposed as a further echo, but it is not needed to explain the form and is therefore kept in Notes rather than promoted to a coequal root.

### #182 Bellossom — Joliflor — キレイハナ (Kireihana)

- Japanese `Kireihana` is `綺麗` (*kirei*, beautiful) + `花` (*hana*, flower).
- French `Joliflor` is `jolie + flore`.
- English `Bellossom` securely exposes `bell + blossom`; French `belle` may add a beauty-related echo but is not uniquely proven.
- No dancing, costume, climate, flower anatomy, or evolutionary context appears in meaning/effect because none is needed to define the displayed words.

**Promotion:** Bellossom was removed from `reference-data.js`; its authoritative facts now live in `generation-ii-data.js`.

### #183 Marill — Marill — マリル (Maril)

This is the batch's most uncertainty-sensitive name.

- Japanese is commonly reconstructed from `鞠` (*mari*, ball) + `瑠璃` (*ruri*, lapis lazuli).
- The phonetic compression is not exact enough to treat that construction as confirmed.
- French and English are commonly reconstructed from `marine + rill`.
- French therefore receives an English-loanword tag on `rill`; English uses its ordinary English word directly.
- The international spelling also closely follows Japanese `Maril`, but no naming account reviewed here establishes whether the international form was primarily adapted from Japanese or independently segmented into marine/rill.

The entry therefore keeps every language at plausible confidence instead of using the Pokémon's appearance, typing, or family to choose the most satisfying theory.

### #184 Azumarill — Azumarill — マリルリ (Marilli)

- Japanese extends the same uncertain `mari + ruri` material proposed for Maril, so it remains plausible.
- French and English more transparently add `azur/azure` to the existing Marill name.
- Spanish/Portuguese `azul` is sometimes suggested as reinforcement, but `azure` already explains the English opening and avoids asserting an undocumented donor language.
- Older `marine + rill` readings are treated as inherited inside `Marill`, rather than pretending Azumarill contains three newly independent roots.

### #185 Sudowoodo — Simularbre — ウソッキー (Usokkie)

- Japanese combines `嘘` (*uso*, lie/falsehood) + `木` (*ki*, tree/wood), with `嘘つき` (*usotsuki*, liar) as an additional sound overlap.
- French `Simularbre` combines `simulacre + arbre`; `simuler` may reinforce the opening.
- English is transparently `pseudo- + wood`.
- All three therefore encode falseness plus tree/wood language directly. The glosses do not need to say that the Pokémon imitates a tree; that is character behavior, while the words themselves already say false/sham wood or tree.

### #186 Politoed — Tarpaud — ニョロトノ (Nyorotono)

- Japanese combines `ニョロニョロ` (*nyoronyoro*, wriggling) + `殿` (*tono*, lord).
- `殿様蛙` (*tonosama-gaeru*, dark-spotted frog) may reinforce the ending and remains explicitly secondary.
- French `Tarpaud` is `têtard + crapaud`.
- English `Politoed` is `polliwog + toad`.
- `poly- + toed` is a plausible secondary English spelling joke rather than the required core.

**Promotion:** Politoed was removed from `reference-data.js`; its authoritative facts now live in `generation-ii-data.js`.

## Naming provenance

Generation II generation-scoped defaults remain appropriate for all eight entries.

No new individual Japanese, French, or English species override was added. Some French etymologies are documented in sources that cite Julien Bardakoff explaining the constructions, but explaining a name's roots is not automatically proof of sole species-level coinage. The repository's Nintendo France team default therefore remains the more precise historical claim.

## Language tags

Only exact displayed borrowed material is tagged:

- Mareep Japanese: English `merry` and `sheep`;
- Ampharos English: Greek `pharos`;
- Bellossom English: possible French `belle`;
- Marill French: English `rill`.

Possible donor languages that cannot be selected confidently are not assigned a tag merely to make the interface look complete.

## Principal sources

Runtime entries carry targeted links. Principal source classes for this batch are:

- PokeAPI source CSVs for current factual types and EV yields;
- Bulbapedia species pages for official Japanese romanizations and structured multilingual name-origin leads;
- Poképédia species pages for French etymologies and localization-specific evidence;
- CNRTL for French lexical definitions;
- Merriam-Webster for English vocabulary and the `pharos`/`ampere` roots.

Community encyclopedia reconstructions are treated as structured evidence, not as permission to erase uncertainty. Dictionary evidence verifies what a proposed root means; it does not by itself prove that a localizer intended the blend.
