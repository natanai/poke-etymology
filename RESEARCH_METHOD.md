# Research and verification method

Each Pokémon entry is reviewed as a set of separate claims rather than receiving one blanket “verified” label.

## What is checked

1. Official English, French, and Japanese names
2. Japanese romanization
3. Types and EV yield
4. Proposed roots and wordplay in each language
5. Literal or approximate meaning
6. Familiar words, phrases, sounds, or cultural references the name may evoke for native speakers
7. The relationship between the three localizations
8. Sources and date of review

## Evidence labels

- **confirmed** — an official creator, publication, game, or other primary source explicitly states the origin
- **strong** — the construction is linguistically transparent and supported by reliable language references and multiple Pokémon references
- **plausible** — the interpretation fits the spelling, sound, and design, but another explanation is also reasonable
- **speculative** — insufficient evidence; these claims should normally be omitted rather than published as fact

A Pokémon may contain both confirmed and uncertain components. The site should state the uncertainty locally instead of averaging everything into one confidence score.

## Source hierarchy

1. Official Pokémon games, Pokédexes, websites, card lists, and creator interviews
2. Official or archival Game Freak material
3. Structured game data such as PokéAPI, checked against a second reference where practical
4. Reputable dictionaries, etymological dictionaries, and language corpora
5. Bulbapedia and similar specialist references as research leads and secondary summaries

Fan theories are never promoted to fact merely because they are repeated widely.

## Native-speaker associations

“May evoke” notes are not claims that every speaker has the same reaction. They should:

- use ordinary, recognizable example words or phrases;
- distinguish a common word from a technical or archaic root;
- explain loanwords and sound symbolism;
- avoid inventing associations solely because two words look vaguely similar;
- use cautious wording when native-speaker review is still needed.

For example, English **-saur** is most readily recognized through words such as **dinosaur** and **tyrannosaur**, rather than as a standalone everyday word.

## Review statuses

- **pending** — factual record exists, but etymology has not been researched
- **draft** — initial research entered; sources or language notes remain incomplete
- **audited** — names, game data, roots, associations, comparison, and sources have been checked
- **native review requested** — structurally audited, but a fluent/native speaker should review nuance

## Batch process

Entries are audited in small evolutionary-family batches. Every batch should:

1. verify factual data;
2. compare existing text against sources;
3. correct overconfident or incomplete claims;
4. add native-association examples;
5. attach sources;
6. record the review date;
7. preserve unresolved alternatives explicitly.

This keeps the site usable while preventing a large amount of polished but unreliable AI-generated etymology from being published at once.
